# Performance Profile — a measurement report

**Analysis only.** No optimisation is proposed here and nothing in the
application was changed to produce these results. This document reports what
each subsystem costs, how that cost scales, and with what confidence.

**Primary dataset:** the paired device run of **2026-08-18**, build `230f179` —
two captures of one session, Low Power Mode on and off as a recorded treatment,
carrying **all three suite tiers** (headless, UI, stress) on physical hardware
for the first time. Conditions in §2.1; what may and may not be compared between
the two captures in §2.2.

**The three headline results.** `analyzeSketch` is **cubic** (k = 3.198), with
its mechanism identified in source and its memory footprint predicted to 2.2 %
(§5.5). Edge enumeration is **quadratic** (k = 2.012) against a control ladder
that does strictly more work and is 200× cheaper (§6.5). A patterned fillet
costs **142.9 ms per occurrence**, of which 97.6 % is edge enumeration (§8.2).

> **§17 supersedes parts of the above.** A round of optimisation has since been
> measured on the device. The cubic is gone — `analyzeSketch` now fits
> **k = 2.285** [2.076, 2.495], non-overlapping with 3.198, and costs 562 ms
> where it cost 8 837. Edge enumeration is **21× cheaper but still quadratic**
> (k = 1.930), and a patterned fillet costs **9.4 ms** per occurrence. Sections
> 1–16 are deliberately left as the pre-optimisation measurement — they are the
> derivation, and §17 reports what changed against them.

**Two claims in earlier revisions of this document were overturned by this run**
and are marked where they appear: Low Power Mode is not a uniform scalar (§3.5),
and the 3D push path costs 0.097 ms rather than the 8.8 ms its round-trip span
reports (§7.2.4).

**If you are here to optimise, read `OPTIMIZATION_PLAN.md` first.** The
measurement phase is finished and the work is split across five parallel
sessions with binding file ownership; that document says which findings are
yours, what you may touch, and how not to destroy the other four sessions'
work. **Do not edit this file** — it is folded from the sessions' own findings
files at integration.

Companion documents: `PERF_ANALYSIS.md` (§8–15, chronological derivation of
each finding), `HANDOFF.md` (entry point). The original experimental design,

## 1. Method

Numbers without a stated method are anecdotes. This section defines how every
value below was obtained and what it can support.

### 1.1 Instrument

Timing is `Perf.span`: a pooled `Stopwatch` around a synchronous body,
recorded via `sw.elapsedMicroseconds / 1000.0` (`perf.dart:372`). Two
consequences follow and govern the whole report:

| Property | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Quantum *q* (tick) | **1 µs = 0.001 ms** |
| Observation domain | integer multiples of *q* |
| Per-probe overhead | one `Stopwatch` start/stop + one map lookup; no allocation |
| Aggregation | count, sum, mean, p50, p95, max, over a 128-sample ring buffer |

Counters (`Perf.count`) and gauges (`Perf.gauge`) are exact integers and carry
no timing uncertainty. **Where a count and a duration answer the same
question, the count is the stronger evidence** — it is exact, and it is
invariant under a change of processor.

### 1.2 Quantization model and resolution classes

Because observations are integer-quantized, a uniform quantization model
applies. For a single observation the quantization standard deviation is

> σ_q = q / √12 = 0.289 µs

and for a mean of *n* independent observations the standard error contributed
by quantization alone is

> SE_q = q / √(12·n)

Define the signal-to-noise ratio of a reported mean as SNR = x̄ / SE_q. Every
span in this run was classified on that basis:

| Class | Criterion | Count (of 273 spans) | Treatment |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| **Resolved** | SNR ≥ 10 | **239** | Reported as a measurement |
| **Marginal** | 3 ≤ SNR < 10 | 12 | Reported with the caveat inline |
| **Unresolved** | SNR < 3 | 22 | Reported as **“< 1 µs”**, never as a number |

This matters. Twenty-two spans in this run — including
`pattern.occurrences.mirror`, `app.sceneRevs`, `app.undoStep`,
`app.redoStep`, `sketch.syncProjections`, `modify.offset`, and seven drawing
tools — produced a mean of 0.00000 ms. That value is **not** a measurement of
zero; it is the statement that every observation fell below one microsecond.
Quoting it as “0.000 ms” would imply a precision the instrument does not have.

### 1.3 Measurement scope — two accumulators, and why they differ

Every timing appears in two places, and confusing them was the source of two
errors in an earlier draft of this document. They are stated here as a
convention that the rest of the report follows.

| Scope | Source | Contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Scenario** | `perf_suite.json`, `perf_suite_ui.json` | Spans recorded *inside one bracketed scenario*, measured pass only |
| **Session** | `perf_snapshot.json` | Every span recorded over the process lifetime, **including the warm-up pass** |

Because `runPerfSuite` executes a warm-up pass before the measured one,
**session counts are approximately twice scenario counts**. This is verified
rather than assumed: `ffi.occt.allEdges` records n = 25 / 6077.70 ms in
scenario scope and n = 50 / 12 163.32 ms in session scope — a ratio of exactly
2.0, with the *means* agreeing to 0.07 % (243.11 ms vs 243.27 ms).

The consequences for reading this report:

* **Means, p50, p95 and maxima are safe in either scope** and are quoted from
  session scope, which has the larger sample.
* **Totals and shares are scenario-scope only.** A share computed from a
  session total against a scenario denominator is meaningless.
* Every share below uses the denominator `ci/perf_report.py` uses: the sum of
  all span totals across both runners, 18 480.63 ms for this run.

### 1.4 Significant figures

Values are reported to **three significant figures, truncated at the
quantization floor** for their sample size. A mean over n = 100 has
SE_q ≈ 2.9 × 10⁻⁵ ms, so four decimal places in ms is the finest defensible
resolution; a single observation (n = 1) supports three.

Sample size *n* is given for every timing statistic in this report. A
statistic without an *n* is not interpretable and none appears here.

### 1.5 Curve fitting

Cost curves are fitted by ordinary least squares on log-transformed axes,
giving the exponent *k* in t ∝ n^k. For each family this report gives:

* **N** — number of distinct sizes measured (not the number of observations);
* ***k*** — the fitted exponent;
* **R²** — fraction of log-variance explained;
* **95 % CI on *k*** — from the standard error of the slope, `t ≈ 1.96`.

Three rules are applied and never silently violated:

1. **N = 2 yields a slope, not a fit.** Two points have zero residual degrees
   of freedom: R² is 1.000 by construction and no confidence interval exists.
   Such families are marked *slope only* and no scaling claim is made from
   them.
2. **A low R² is a result, not a failure.** Where the data do not support a
   power law, that is reported as the finding rather than a number being
   quoted anyway.
3. **A wide CI forbids a claim.** Where the interval spans linear and
   quadratic, the report says the two cannot be distinguished.

The size axis differs per family (profile points, entity count, feature count,
edge count) and is stated with each table. The dependent variable is the
scenario’s **dominant span total** — the largest single span recorded inside
the scenario — which excludes fixture construction. This is the same metric
`ci/perf_report.py` uses, so any table here can be regenerated.

### 1.6 Verdict criteria

Three verdicts are used, defined numerically rather than by impression:

| Verdict | Criteria (both required) |
| --- | --- |
| **SMOOTH** | Cost < 1 ms at the largest measured size, **and** upper CI bound on *k* ≤ 1.2 |
| **WATCH** | Cost currently tolerable, **but** lower CI bound on *k* > 1.2 |
| **PROBLEM** | Cost > 10 ms at a size reachable in ordinary use, at any *k* |

Where a verdict rests on judgement beyond these thresholds, the judgement is
stated in the same sentence.

### 1.7 Known confounds

Stated up front because each one limits what the data can support.

1. **Low Power Mode was active for the entire run** (verified at both ends).
   The CPU is capped by policy. A prior controlled comparison — builds
   `7fb7f8b` (LPM off) and `9bfe397` (LPM on), same suite — measured a uniform
   1.67–2.05× slowdown across four unrelated subsystems, so **absolute times
   here are approximately 2× pessimistic** for this device at full clock.
   Ratios, exponents and counts are unaffected. This condition is **not only a
   confound**: §3.5 shows it is a usable proxy for slower hardware, and states
   the limits of that reading.
2. **Self-interference.** The suite executes synchronous FFI calls on the UI
   thread for 24 s. Frame statistics for the session are therefore *of the
   measurement*, not of the application at rest (§3.4).
3. **Warm-up.** Fixtures are memoised and a warm-up pass runs before
   measurement, but the first rung of a ramp still carries more construction
   cost than later rungs. Where this is visible it is noted.
4. **Memoisation.** `gearCurve` memoises on full geometric identity; the cold
   path is measured only because the scenario explicitly clears the cache
   between calls. Any operation with an undisclosed cache is a systematic risk
   to this kind of measurement, and this one was found the hard way (M212).
5. **Single-device.** All results are from one iPad. Nothing here supports a
   claim about any other chip except through the exponents.

### 2.2 The two captures share one app session — what may and may not be compared

Both bundles report the same perf-session epoch, `2026-08-18T18:43:16.543648`,
and the native uptime counter runs 1360 s → 1420 s (A) → 1530 s → 1571 s (B).
**They are two captures from a single continuous app session, 170 s apart, A
first.** This has two consequences that govern every comparison below.

1. **Suite-scope data are independent and may be compared directly.** Each
   capture re-executes all three suites from scratch, so `perf_suite*.json` in A
   and B are two independent measurements of the same scripted work. All
   Low Power Mode ratios in §3.5, and every stress ladder in §6.5, rest on this.

2. **Session-scope data are cumulative and may *not* be differenced.** The
   `perf_snapshot.json` accumulator is never reset, so B's session totals
   strictly *contain* A's. B reports 3165 frames to A's 657 not because the
   second capture saw more interaction, but because it counts A's frames too.
   Session-scope numbers are therefore quoted from **B only**, as the whole
   session, and never subtracted.

A side effect of (1) worth recording, because it misleads on first reading: the
gauge map is not cleared between suite executions, so the *earlier* scenarios of
capture B carry leftover gauge values from capture A's stress pass. Each
ladder's own gauges are read from its own scenario entry, where they are written
by the pass being measured.

Three things this table establishes, each of which a result below depends on:

- **Thermal state is `nominal` at all four sample points.** No time-dependent
  throttling drift; the second half of each run is comparable with the first,
  and A is comparable with B.
- **Low Power Mode is recorded, not inferred.** The native probe reports
  `lowPowerMode` explicitly and it is stable across each suite (pre = post), so

  experiment with a known treatment, not a post-hoc attribution.
- **Low Power Mode left all 9 cores active.** `activeProcessorCount` is 9 in
  both arms, so the treatment is a *frequency* cap, not core parking. This
  matters for §3.5: it predicts a per-class clock scalar rather than a
  parallelism loss, and the app is in any case single-threaded on the UI thread
  (§9.1).

Peak resident memory reaches 850 MB against 7374 MB physical with 3437 MB still
available at the worst point, so **no result in this document is confounded by
memory pressure** — every ladder that stopped, stopped on time (§6.5).

### 3.2 Repeatability — the noise floor

`quality.variance` re-executes four representative operations and reports
dispersion. Without this, any run-to-run difference is uninterpretable.

| Operation | Median | IQR | Full spread | Smallest meaningful difference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `extrude` | 2834 µs | **0 %** | 3 % | ~3 % |
| `solve` | 281 µs | 4 % | 18 % | ~18 % |
| `analyze` | 1777 µs | 13 % | 17 % | ~17 % |
| `splineEval` | 104 µs | 7 % | **67 %** | ~67 % |

Kernel work is effectively noise-free. **A change below ~17 % in `analyze` or
`solve`, or below ~67 % in `splineEval`, is not a signal.** This is the
threshold against which any future baseline diff must be read.

### 3.3 Null-measurement audit

A scenario that reaches nothing still emits a number, and a fast zero is
indistinguishable from a fast operation. Guards are therefore explicit:

| Counter | Value | Status |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `kernel.sweepTwist.fail` | 2 | **Explained, and not a defect — see below.** |
| `tool.build.fillet.null` | 1 | Expected: the generic point generator cannot guarantee a fittable corner. Covered by `tools.fillet2d`. |
| `tool.build.chamfer.null` | 1 | Expected, same cause. |

Identical in both arms of the paired run.

#### A question open across three device runs, closed on the first outing of `Perf.note`

`kernel.sweepTwist` returned null on every previous device run, and the reason
was unobtainable: the shim logged it, but `log.txt` is captured roughly 25 s
*before* the suite executes, so the explanation was never in the bundle. §9.2
recorded this as a defect in the apparatus. M221 added `Perf.note`, a string
channel carried inside the suite JSON itself, and the fix worked on its first
device run. Both bundles carry:

```
kernel.sweepTwist.fail.reason = "occt_sweep_profile: twist is not implemented yet"
```

**The null is a correct report of an unimplemented kernel feature, not a
failure of the measurement or of the geometry.** No performance conclusion was
ever drawn from it, and none is now; the value of the result is that a
three-run-old ambiguity is closed and one item can leave §9.2.

The two remaining nulls, `tool.build.fillet.null` and
`tool.build.chamfer.null`, emit no note because the null originates in the Dart
tool builder rather than the shim. They remain explained by construction rather
than by instrument.

All other scenarios published a non-zero subject gauge. The M220 fixtures were
verified to reach their subject on-device: face decomposition returned
26/122/362 surfaces at the three sizes, attribution matched 62 faces, and the
five pattern kinds produced 15/15/16/15/1 placements — each equal to the value
its host-side test asserts. The M221 fixtures likewise:
`app.blendPattern.edgesFound = 180` at all three occurrence counts (§8.2), and
every stress ladder published a non-zero `maxSize` (§6.5).

### 3.4 What the frame counters do and do not measure

Session scope, capture B (the whole session): n = 3165 frames, 201 over 33 ms
(**6.35 %**), headline 29.5 fps.

| Phase | n | mean | p50 † | p95 † | max | session total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `frame.build` (Dart) | 3165 | 0.658 ms | 0.135 ms | 0.519 ms | 18.7 ms | 2 084 ms |
| `frame.raster` (GPU) | 3165 | 2.056 ms | 2.402 ms | 3.067 ms | 61.3 ms | 6 507 ms |
| `frame.total` | 3165 | 33.88 ms | 12.638 ms | 13.501 ms | **59 844 ms** | 107 244 ms |

† **The percentiles describe only the last 128 frames.** Percentiles come from a
128-sample ring buffer (`perf.dart:41`) while `n`, `mean` and `total` cover every
observation. The two columns therefore answer different questions and must not
be combined into one distribution. The reduced-clock arm makes the hazard
visible: capture A reports mean 22.19 ms against p50 34.83 ms — a mean *below*
the median, which is impossible within one sample and merely reflects that A's
last 128 frames were its most expensive.

Three findings follow, and they are the reason this section is no longer purely
a caveat:

1. **Flutter's own work is not the cost.** Build + raster total 8 591 ms against
   107 244 ms of frame time — **8.0 %**. The remaining **92.0 %** is the UI
   thread blocked inside synchronous FFI (§1.7 confound 2, §9.1). This closure
   figure independently reproduces the ~92 % obtained on the earlier run (§12.2),
   on a session with ten times the frame count.
2. **One frame accounts for more than half of all frame time.** The worst frame
   is 59 844 ms — 55.8 % of the session's entire 107 244 ms. That single frame is
   the stress tier's `allEdges` and `analyze` ladders blocking the UI thread, and
   it is what drags the mean to 33.88 ms.
3. **The headline fps figure is an artefact and must not be quoted.** Capture A
   reports **45.1 fps** and capture B **29.5 fps** — the *reduced-clock* arm
   scores better, because B's session accumulator also contains B's own
   longer stress pass. Frames-per-second computed over a session that includes
   deliberate multi-second blocking work measures the suite, not the app.

These figures characterise the instrument under load. The only usability
statement they support is the negative one in (1): whatever is making frames
long, it is not Flutter's build or raster phase.

### 3.5 Low Power Mode as a proxy for older hardware

Low Power Mode is now a **designed** controlled experiment rather than an
observed nuisance: the paired captures of §2.1 differ in the recorded
`lowPowerMode` flag and in nothing else — same device, same build, same session,
170 s apart, thermal `nominal` throughout, all 9 cores active in both arms. The
app must also run on iPads considerably slower than an M4, so the question is
what transform the cap applies: a uniform clock scaling, or a change in the
machine's balance.

Earlier runs answered this from **four** hand-picked spans. The paired run
answers it from **142 matched spans across 187 scenarios**, and the answer is
more interesting than the earlier one — and in one respect corrects it.

#### The scalar itself

Estimator: for every span appearing in both arms with an identical call count
`n`, the ratio of mean durations; aggregated as a geometric mean, since ratios
compose multiplicatively. Spans below a duration cut are excluded so that the
1 µs quantum (§1.2) cannot dominate.

| Duration cut | n spans | geometric mean | 95 % CI | CV |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: |
| ≥ 0.5 ms | 217 | 1.9361 | [1.9142, 1.9582] | 9.4 % |
| ≥ 1 ms | 177 | 1.9351 | [1.9100, 1.9606] | 9.8 % |
| **≥ 2 ms** | **142** | **1.9253** | **[1.8979, 1.9531]** | **9.4 %** |
| ≥ 5 ms | 95 | 1.9389 | [1.9051, 1.9733] | 9.5 % |
| ≥ 10 ms | 71 | 1.9362 | [1.9026, 1.9704] | 8.3 % |
| ≥ 25 ms | 45 | 1.9188 | [1.8894, 1.9487] | 5.2 % |
| ≥ 50 ms | 23 | 1.9175 | [1.8814, 1.9544] | 4.6 % |

The estimate is **stable to within 1.1 % across a hundredfold change in the
duration cut** (1.918–1.939), and the dispersion falls monotonically as the cut
rises (9.4 % → 4.6 %) — the signature of a constant multiplier observed through
shrinking measurement noise. Aggregating instead at whole-scenario granularity
gives 1.958 [1.899, 2.018] over 180 scenarios, consistent with the span-level
figure. The earlier four-point estimate of 1.893 lies **1.7 %** from the
142-span figure of 1.9253.

#### Ruling out warm-up

Because A and B are consecutive captures in one session with the *slower* arm
first, a rival explanation must be excluded: that B is faster merely because it
ran second, on warmed caches and settled allocators. This is testable — a
warm-up effect decays with execution position, a clock scalar does not. Fitting
log(ratio) against scenario execution order over the 180 paired scenarios:

| Quantity | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Slope | **+0.000095 per scenario** |
| Standard error | 0.000301 |
| 95 % CI | **[−0.000495, +0.000686]** — contains zero |
| R² | **0.0006** |
| Implied ratio at first scenario | 1.941 |
| Implied ratio at last scenario | 1.974 |

The slope is statistically indistinguishable from zero, explains 0.06 % of the
variance, and is *positive* — the opposite sign to a decaying warm-up. Over the
full 180-scenario sequence the fitted ratio moves by +1.7 %. **Warm-up is
excluded as the explanation.** The suites also run their own warm-up pass before
the measured pass (§1.3), so both arms were already warm.

#### The correction: the scalar is *not* uniform across the machine

The earlier four-point result concluded that Low Power Mode "behaves as a clock
scalar, not as a different machine", on the strength of one memory-bound and one
compute-bound span agreeing to 0.5 %. **With 142 spans that conclusion is too
strong and is hereby narrowed.** Grouping by what the work actually is:

| Class | n | geometric mean | 95 % CI |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `ffi.occt.meshCreate` — bulk buffer copy-out, DRAM-bound | 7 | **1.624** | range 1.578–1.741 |
| Native FFI overall (C++ across the boundary) | 71 | 1.9101 | [1.885, 1.936] |
| Mixed / other | 59 | 1.8792 | [1.851, 1.908] |
| **Dart compute** (solver, constraints, DOF analysis) | 12 | **2.2729** | [2.091, 2.471] |

Dart compute against native FFI: Welch t = 3.86, df = 11.5 (**p ≈ 0.002**),
ratio-of-ratios **1.190×**. The confidence intervals do not overlap. The extremes
are `constraints.add.dimension` at 2.91× and `solve.lm` at 2.74× against
`ffi.occt.meshCreate` at 1.58×.

The physical reading is consistent and unsurprising once seen: Low Power Mode
caps **core frequency** (§3.1 confirms it parks no cores). Compute-bound work
scales with frequency almost exactly; work rate-limited by DRAM latency and
bandwidth does not, because memory timing is not clocked down with the core.
`ffi.occt.meshCreate` — a bulk copy of vertex and index buffers across the FFI
boundary — is the purest memory-bound span in the suite, and it is the least
affected by the cap, tightly and repeatably (7 observations spanning
1.578–1.741).

**So the corrected statement is:** Low Power Mode is a good proxy, and a
*stratified* one. A single scalar of 1.93 describes the app as a whole to within
about ±10 %, but any extrapolation of a *specific* subsystem must use its class:
**≈1.62 for memory-bound bulk transfer, ≈1.91 for native geometry work, ≈2.27
for Dart compute.** The earlier claim of uniformity was an artefact of a
four-point sample that happened to draw both its anchors from classes with
similar scalars.

**Why this proxy is unusually valid for this application.** Finding B1 of the
original survey still holds: there is not a single `Isolate` in the codebase,
and all 58 FFI entry points execute synchronously on the UI thread. The app is
therefore clock-bound and effectively single-threaded on its critical path, so
core count is nearly irrelevant to it and clock is nearly everything — exactly
the axis Low Power Mode moves. For a parallel application this proxy would be
much weaker.

#### What it does and does not stand in for

| Axis of an older iPad | Proxied? | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lower CPU clock | **Yes, well** | 1.9253 [1.8979, 1.9531] over 142 spans, stable across a 100× range of duration cuts; warm-up excluded (slope ≈ 0, R² = 0.0006) |
| Single-thread-bound critical path | **Yes** | Zero isolates; all 58 FFI entry points on the UI thread. LPM parked no cores (9 active in both arms), so the treatment moves exactly the axis this app is bound by |
| 60 Hz panel rather than ProMotion | **Yes, incidentally** | LPM caps refresh; §8.6 gives the 60 Hz budget |
| Smaller caches / lower memory bandwidth | **No — and now measured to be so** | Memory-bound work is the *least* affected class (1.624 for `meshCreate` against 2.273 for Dart compute, p ≈ 0.002). An older SoC would move memory in the *same* direction as the core, so the cap systematically **under**-predicts the penalty on memory-bound paths |
| **Less RAM, lower jetsam ceiling** | **No** | This device had 3.4–3.9 GB of headroom throughout. A 3–4 GB iPad has far less |
| Weaker GPU | **Not tested** | `frame.raster` p95 was 3.07 ms here and is not GPU-limited |

The unproxied memory axis is the consequential one: **the field crash was a
memory kill on `phys_footprint`, and Low Power Mode says nothing whatsoever
about that.** The two figures that do transfer unchanged, because they are
properties of the data rather than of the processor, are **14 bytes per
triangle** and **2 KB per solid** (§8.5). On a 4 GB iPad those convert directly
into a much lower ceiling on model size.

#### What a slower device can still handle

For a cost model t = c·n^e, holding wall time fixed, the reachable size on a
device k times slower scales as k^(−1/e). Taking k = **1.9253**, the measured
scalar, and using the exponents measured on the stress ladders of the same run
(§6.5, §5.5) rather than the earlier ramp estimates:

| Operation | e (measured, this run) | Reachable size vs the faster device |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `analyzeSketch` (DOF analysis) | **3.198** | **81.5 %** |
| `allEdges` (edge enumeration) | 2.012 | 72.2 % |
| Sketch drag | 1.922 | 71.1 % |
| Constraint solve | 1.868 | 70.4 % |
| Boolean chain | 1.574 | 66.0 % |
| Build + mesh + extrude | 1.063 | 54.0 % |

**A non-obvious consequence: steeper exponents lose *less* reachable size.**
Because a cubic cost climbs fast, only a modest reduction in size is needed to
absorb a given slowdown, whereas a linear operation must nearly halve its input
to absorb a factor of two. This inverts the intuition that superlinear
operations are the ones that "fall off a cliff" on weaker hardware — on the
*size* axis they degrade most gracefully. What makes them dangerous is not their
behaviour across devices but their behaviour across model sizes on any one
device.

Applying this to the dominant 3D defect — the model size at which `allEdges`
alone consumes one second. The model is fitted to the stress ladder's nominal
arm (360 edges → 616 ms, e = 2.012) and reproduces all three measured rungs to
within 0.9 % (616 / 2485 / 10021 ms modelled against 616 / 2508 / 10017 measured):

| Device state | 1-second threshold |
| --- | ---: |
| **This device, LPM off (measured)** | **≈ 458 edges** (≈ 153 profile points) |
| This device, LPM on (measured) | ≈ 331 edges (≈ 110 profile points) |
| A device a further 1.93× slower | ≈ 239 edges (≈ 80 profile points) |

These three thresholds were derived on the earlier run, from the *ramp* tier and
a different fitted exponent, as 456 / 328 / 236 edges. The stress tier
reproduces them to **0.5 %, 0.9 % and 1.3 %** from independent data on a
different build. A part with a few hundred edges is unremarkable. On slower
hardware the defect of §6.5 is not merely slower — it is reached by smaller
models.

#### Standing recommendation

Because the transform is a well-characterised per-class scalar and the
application is clock-bound, **paired runs are worth taking deliberately**, not
merely tolerated when they happen. The protocol of §15.2 — one capped capture,
one uncapped, same session, same build — is now validated: it produced a scalar
with a 1.4 % confidence interval, excluded its main rival explanation, and
revealed a subsystem dependence that four points had hidden. It should be the
standing protocol for every future device run.

Two gaps a capped run cannot close:

- **Memory.** This device had 3.4–3.9 GB of headroom throughout. A capped clock
  says nothing about a 3–4 GB iPad's jetsam ceiling. The transferable figures
  are the ones that are properties of the data rather than the processor — **14
  bytes per triangle** and **2 KB per solid** (§8.5) — plus the newly measured
  **+105 MB for a single 1024-entity DOF analysis** (§5.5), which is the largest
  single allocation the suite has ever provoked and scales as O(n²).
- **Memory-bound work specifically.** Now that the cap is known to
  *under*-represent the penalty on DRAM-bound paths (1.62 against 2.27), any
  estimate for older hardware on those paths should be treated as optimistic.

#### 5.5.2 Why it is cubic — the mechanism, and a quantitative check

This is not merely an empirical curve; the exponent is a property of the
algorithm, visible in `solver.dart:2470`. For a sketch of `total` parameters and
`m` residuals:

1. **Dense Jacobian by finite differences** (`solver.dart:2506–2516`). For each
   of `total` columns, one full `_residuals` evaluation costing O(m), written
   into a dense `m × total` matrix. Cost O(m · total) — quadratic.
2. **Rank and pivots by reduction to RREF** (`solver.dart:2517`). Gaussian
   elimination on that dense matrix. Cost **O(m · total · rank)** — **cubic.**
3. **Null-space basis** (`solver.dart:2530–2544`), one vector per free column
   over the pivot rows. Cost O(total · rank) — quadratic.

The fixture makes the constants exact. At the top rung, `sketchFixture(512)`
produces 512 circles (3 parameters each) and 512 lines (4 each), so
**total = 512·3 + 512·4 = 3584** parameters; `constraintFixture(512)` produces
511 `equal` + 1024 `coincident` + `fix` + `dimension` = **1537 constraints**,
matching the recorded gauge exactly, contributing **m = 511·1 + 1024·2 + 2 + 1 =
2562** residuals. The system is of full row rank, so rank = m = 2562 and

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;dof = total − rank = 3584 − 2562 = **1022**

against the recorded gauge `analyze.dof = 1022`. **The arithmetic closes
exactly**, which confirms the fixture is producing the system the model assumes.

Two independent predictions follow, and both are confirmed by the measurement:

**Prediction 1 — step 2 dominates.** Jacobian construction costs
total · m = 9.18 × 10⁶ operations; RREF costs m · total · rank = 2.35 × 10¹⁰ —
a ratio of **2562 : 1**. The cubic term should therefore be the whole cost at
this size, and the fitted exponent should be ≈ 3. Measured: 3.198 [2.835, 3.561].
The implied throughput, 2.35 × 10¹⁰ operations in 8 837 ms, is 2.66 × 10⁹
elementary double operations per second — a plausible rate for Dart arithmetic
over nested `List<double>`.

**Prediction 2 — the memory footprint is the dense matrix.** The allocation is
dominated by two structures:

| Structure | Dimensions | Bytes |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Dense Jacobian `j` | 2562 × 3584 × 8 B | 73.5 MB |
| Null-space basis | 1022 × 3584 × 8 B | 29.3 MB |
| **Predicted total** | | **102.8 MB** |
| **Measured `stress.analyze.rssDeltaMB`** | | **105 MB** |

**Agreement to 2.2 %.** The recorded RSS delta of a single DOF analysis is
accounted for, to within measurement resolution, by the dense matrices the
algorithm is documented to build. The reduced-clock arm records +315 MB for the
same rung; that difference is garbage-collector scheduling, not a different
allocation — the structural cost is the 105 MB figure, and it grows as O(n²).

Three independent lines of evidence, each capable of refuting the others.

**Observed cost.**

| --- | ---: | ---: |

| `ffi.occt.edgeInfo.calls` (exact counter) | — | **6552** |

Per §1.3: the two scopes differ by the warm-up pass (ratio exactly 2.0) and
their means agree to 0.07 %. The share is scenario-scope against the
18 480.63 ms denominator; distribution statistics are session-scope.

**Evidence 1 — growth curve.** `ramp.allEdges`, N = 7,
**k = 1.935, R² = 0.99978, 95 % CI [1.910, 1.960]**

| Profile pts | Edges | ms | local exponent | µs per edge |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 12 | ≈ 36 | 14.1 | — | 392 |
| 24 | ≈ 72 | 50.8 | 1.85 | 706 |
| 36 | ≈ 108 | 111 | 1.93 | 1029 |
| 48 | ≈ 144 | 196 | 1.97 | 1361 |
| 72 | ≈ 216 | 434 | 1.96 | 2008 |
| 96 | ≈ 288 | 766 | 1.98 | 2659 |
| 144 | ≈ 432 | **1709** | 1.98 | **3955** |

R² = 0.9998 over a 12× range, with local exponents converging on 1.98 and
remaining there. This is a clean quadratic with no knee. Per-edge cost rises
10× across the ramp — the diagnostic signature of per-call work proportional
to the whole shape.

**Evidence 2 — one call against a growing shape.** `kernel.query.edgeInfoScale`
queries **the same edge** (index 1), 20 times, on solids of increasing size.
The requested work is held constant; only the surrounding shape varies.

N = 4, **k = 0.99, R² = 0.9999, 95 % CI [0.97, 1.01]**

| Profile pts | Edges | Faces | mean per call (n = 40) | ratio to smallest |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 24 | 72 | 26 | 0.6252 ms | 1.00 |
| 60 | 180 | 62 | 1.5080 ms | 2.41 |
| 120 | 360 | 122 | 3.0383 ms | 4.86 |
| 240 | 720 | 242 | **6.0729 ms** | **9.71** |

Edge and face counts are exact gauges read from the kernel, not derived.

Edge count ×10 → cost of one unchanged query ×9.71, with the exponent’s CI
excluding anything outside [0.97, 1.01]. **A single `edgeInfo` is Θ(shape).**
`allEdges` issues one per edge; n × Θ(n) = Θ(n²) then follows arithmetically
rather than by inference. A flat line here would have refuted the hypothesis
and returned the diagnosis to the FFI boundary; it did not appear.

> **Interpretation warning.** `ci/perf_report.py` labels this family “linear”.
> For this family linear is the *positive* result for the defect hypothesis,
> because the swept axis is not the quantity of work requested but the size of
> the shape one fixed unit of work must traverse. The tool cannot distinguish
> the two; a reader must.

**Evidence 3 — control queries on the same solid** (360 edges):

| Query | n | mean | ratio |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `kernel.edgeInfo1` (one edge) | 20 | 2.9862 ms | 1.00 |
| `kernel.counts()` | 20 | 0.2061 ms | **1/14.5** |
| `kernel.bbox()` | 20 | 0.1651 ms | **1/18.1** |

Touching the shape is cheap; crossing the FFI boundary is cheap; querying one
edge is not. **Closure check:** 360 × 2.9862 ms = 1075.0 ms against the measured
`allEdges` of 1169.7 ms on the same solid (`kernel.allEdges.sweep.120`) —
**91.9 % of the total accounted for by per-call cost**, the residual 8.1 %
being boundary crossings and Dart-side list construction. Both figures are
scenario-scope, so the comparison is like-for-like.

**Mechanism in source** (`backend/occt/shim/occt_capi.cpp`): each call performs
four whole-shape operations — `TopExp::MapShapes` (:1738),
`TopExp::MapShapesAndAncestors` (:1792), `BRepBndLib::Add` (:1832), and
construction of a `BRepClass3d_SolidClassifier` (:1836) — and discards all
four. The latter two lie in the convexity branch, taken for any edge with
exactly two adjacent faces: on a closed solid, the majority.

**Evidence 4 — the isolation, with a control that does strictly more work.**
The stress tier (device, 2026-08-18) runs two ladders over **identical solids**,
`extrudeProfileArcs(ringProfile(n, 40), 10.0)`, differing only in what is done
with each:

| Ladder | Work performed per rung |
| --- | --- |
| `stress.kernel.allEdges` | build → read `edgeCount` → **`allEdges()`** |
| `stress.kernel.buildOnly` | build → `counts()` → **`mesh(linDeflection: 0.2)`** |

The control is not a stripped-down comparison — it *tessellates the whole solid*,
which is more downstream work than the subject performs. Reference arm
(LPM off), matched sizes only, never mixing clocks:

| Profile pts | Edges | `allEdges` | `buildOnly` | ratio |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 120 | 360 | 616 ms | 13 ms | 47.4× |
| 240 | 720 | 2 508 ms | 24 ms | 104.5× |
| 480 | 1 440 | **10 017 ms** | 50 ms | **200.3×** |
| 960 | 2 880 | *budget exceeded* | 107 ms | — |
| 1920 | 5 760 | *budget exceeded* | **245 ms** | — |

Reduced-clock arm, same structure: 56.4× at 120 pts and 114.0× at 240 pts.

Four things follow, none of which requires inference:

1. **The ratio grows with size** (47× → 105× → 200×), which is what
   distinguishes a difference in *exponent* from a difference in constant.
2. **The exponents separate cleanly.** `allEdges` fits **k = 2.012, R² = 1.0000,
   CI [1.910, 2.113]** (reference arm) and **2.015** on the reduced-clock arm
   independently. `buildOnly` fits **k = 1.063, R² = 0.9972, CI [0.959, 1.167]**
   and 1.081. The intervals do not overlap; the control is linear and the
   subject is quadratic.
3. **The control reaches four times the size for 2.4 % of the time.**
   `buildOnly` completed 1920 profile points (5 760 edges) in 245 ms, while
   `allEdges` could not pass 480 points. Building and tessellating a solid four
   times larger is 41× cheaper than enumerating the edges of the smaller one.
4. **A lower bound free of any model.** Since the build is common to both
   ladders, build ≤ 50 ms at 480 points, so pure enumeration there is
   **≥ 9 967 ms**. No fit is involved in that statement.

**Compositional closure.** Evidence 2 gives the per-call exponent as
**0.985** (N = 4, R² = 0.9998, both arms identical to three decimals). One call
per edge adds exactly 1, predicting **1.985** for `allEdges`. Measured on a
different tier with different geometry: **2.012 [1.910, 2.113]**. The prediction
lies inside the interval, and the two agree to **1.3 %**. The mechanism is
therefore not merely consistent with the growth curve — it *quantitatively
reproduces its exponent*.

**The wall is time, not memory.** `stress.allEdges.rssDeltaMB` is **0 MB**
across the whole ladder (1 MB on the reduced-clock arm). The ladder stopped
because a rung exceeded the 4 000 ms budget, with 3.9 GB of headroom unused.

**Extrapolation to the part that crashed in the field (≈ 3400 edges),** now
based on the stress ladder's reference arm, with its interval propagated from
the fit and no Low Power Mode correction required — this arm was uncapped:

| Basis | Predicted `allEdges` at 3400 edges |
| --- | ---: |
| CI lower (k = 1.910) | 44.9 s |
| **Central (k = 2.012)** | **56.4 s** |
| CI upper (k = 2.113) | 70.8 s |

The previous estimate, derived from the ramp tier under Low Power Mode and then
corrected by the clock scalar, was "an order of 40–55 s on an uncapped device".
The stress tier measures it **directly on an uncapped device** at 56.4 s
[44.9, 70.8]. Three routes — ramp plus clock correction, per-call composition,
and direct stress ladder — now agree.

**Verdict: PROBLEM.** The second-largest cost in the application, behind
`analyzeSketch` (§5.5) on exponent, and the largest single consumer of measured
span time in the suite at 29.6 %.

### 7.2 Beyond the platform-view boundary — and a correction

The earlier run had a single observation of each native phase. This run has
1 698 camera pushes, 1 698 overlay pushes and 63 scene pushes, which changes
what can be said and **reverses one earlier reading**.

#### 7.2.1 An instrument caveat specific to these spans

`rv.native.*` values are drained from `RvPerf.swift` and re-recorded on the Dart
side as **`n` copies of the native average** (`bug_capture.dart:328`), because
feeding one fat sample would corrupt the call count. Two consequences, both
load-bearing:

- **`p50` and `p95` are meaningless for `rv.native.*` spans** — they are
  percentiles over synthesised copies of a mean. Only `n`, `total` and `mean`
  are exact.
- The true native maximum is published separately as a **gauge**,
  `<name>.worstUs` (`bug_capture.dart:338`), because a sample would corrupt the
  total. That gauge is reset by each drain, so it reports the worst *since the
  previous capture*.

No such caveat applies to the Dart-side `rv.*` spans, which are ordinary
measurements.

#### 7.2.2 First-call construction cost — now measured, not inferred

The drain in capture A covers the session up to 18:44:43, which includes the
first scene ever pushed. Its worst-case gauges isolate that first call:

| Phase | worst in A | worst in B (steady state) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `rv.native.setScene` | **419.67 ms** | 12.20 ms |
| └─ `rv.native.planes` | **419.47 ms** | 8.62 ms |
| `rv.native.solids` | 10.02 ms | 6.22 ms |
| `rv.native.sketches` | 7.61 ms | 4.33 ms |
| `rv.native.accents` | 21.42 ms | 7.12 ms |

**The single most expensive scene push in the session took 419.67 ms, of which
419.47 ms — 99.95 % — was the three origin planes.** The earlier run's n = 1
observation (33.4 ms of a 33.5 ms push, 99.6 %) was directionally right and an
order of magnitude low on the absolute figure. Mesh upload is not the cost of
first displaying a part; RealityKit entity and material construction for the
origin planes is.

#### 7.2.3 Steady state, and the boundary decomposition

Because capture B's session accumulator contains A's (§2.2), the reference-arm
steady state is recovered by differencing B against A. That increment is 1 352
camera pushes and 31 scene pushes taken with the clock uncapped and the planes
already constructed:

| Push | Round trip (Dart) | Native portion | Native share | **Boundary + queueing** |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `setCamera` | 4.654 ms | 0.822 ms | 17.7 % | **82.3 %** |
| `setOverlays` | 4.619 ms | 0.126 ms | 2.7 % | **97.3 %** |
| `setScene` | 18.795 ms | 7.987 ms | 42.5 % | 57.5 % |

Steady-state native scene phases, reference arm: `solids` 2.487 ms, `planes`
2.373 ms, `sketches` 1.252 ms, `accents` 1.624 ms, `placeCamera` 0.013 ms,
`highlight` < 1 µs. Once constructed, the origin planes cost **2.373 ms**
against **419.47 ms** on the first call — a 177-fold amortisation, which is why
§7.2.2's figure must never be quoted as a recurring cost.

#### 7.2.4 The correction: the round trip is latency, not work

The whole-session table reports `rv.setCamera` at n = 1 698, total 14 966 ms,
and `rv.setOverlays` at 14 917 ms. Summed, that is 29.9 seconds, which invites
the conclusion that the orbit path costs half a minute of the session. **That
conclusion would be wrong**, and three independent facts refute it:

1. **The synchronous cost is measured separately and is 0.097 ms** (§7.1).
   `_invoke` times *across an await* on an asynchronous method channel
   (`reality_view.dart:59–64`), and the caller does not await it
   (`viewport3d.dart:430`). The UI thread is never blocked for these 8.8 ms.
2. **The distribution is that of a queue, not of work.** `rv.setCamera` has
   mean 8.814 ms against **p50 1.58 ms** — a mean 5.6× its median, with p95
   16.64 ms and a 421.88 ms tail. Work has a central tendency; queueing delay
   has exactly this shape.
3. **It scales with load, not with the clock.** Between the arms the round trip
   moves by **5.39×** (`setCamera`) and **5.43×** (`setOverlays`) — nearly three
   times the 1.9253 clock scalar of §3.5, and far outside its confidence
   interval. Its native component moves by 2.57×, and `setScene`'s round trip by
   1.97×. A quantity that grows 5.4× when the machine slows 1.93× is not doing
   1.93× more work; it is waiting behind something else.

**What the round-trip figure therefore is:** an independent measurement of how
long the UI thread stays busy with *other* work. The reply cannot be delivered
until the event loop is free, so the tail on `rv.setCamera` is a *symptom* of
the synchronous FFI blocking established in §3.4 (92 % of frame time) and §6.5
— measured through a completely separate channel. It corroborates the principal
finding rather than adding a new cost.

**Verdict: SMOOTH** for the push path itself (0.097 ms synchronous), **WATCH**
for first-scene construction (419 ms, once per part). The 29.9 s of round-trip
latency is reclassified as evidence for §6.5, not as a cost of the display path.

#### The one place patterns are not free — and it is not the arithmetic

`applyBlendOccurrence` (`part_model.dart:6177`) is how a **patterned fillet or
chamfer** is placed at each occurrence. Its first statement is:

```dart
final live = kernel.edgesOf(body);        // :6179
```

and `OcctPartKernel.edgesOf` (`:5245`) is `shape.allEdges()` — the Θ(n²)
operation of §6.5, unmodified. **A patterned blend therefore performs a full
`allEdges` traversal once per occurrence.**

#### It is now measured, and the prediction holds

The M221 scenarios ran on a device for the first time in the paired run of
2026-08-18. **The composition is no longer an inference.**

`app.blendPattern.edgeQuery.{2,4,8}` performs one full `edgesOf` per occurrence
on a body whose edge count is published as a gauge:
`app.blendPattern.edgesFound = 180` at every occurrence count — non-zero, so no
early return is being timed.

| 2 | 285.98 ms | 142.99 ms | 553.34 ms | 276.67 ms |
| 4 | 571.68 ms | 142.92 ms | 1 111.21 ms | 277.80 ms |
| 8 | **1 142.49 ms** | 142.81 ms | **2 213.52 ms** | 276.69 ms |

| Arm | N | *k* | R² | 95 % CI |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |

**Exactly linear in occurrence count, with a per-occurrence cost constant to
0.13 %** across a fourfold range. The two arms differ by 276.9 / 142.9 =
**1.938**, within the confidence interval of the 1.9253 clock scalar (§3.5).

**The composition check the scenario was built for.** If the per-occurrence
cost really is one unmodified `allEdges`, it must equal `kernel.allEdges.sweep`
evaluated at the same edge count. That family fits k = 1.944 on the same arm and
predicts **157.0 ms** for a 180-edge body. Measured: **142.9 ms**, a ratio of
**0.910** — agreement to 9 % between two independently instrumented paths on
different fixtures. The residual is in the expected direction: `edgesOf` returns
a length where the sweep constructs the full Dart-side list.

**The other half of the cost, and how small it is.** `app.patternRebuild.8`
drives a real `PatternFeature` through `recomputeAllFeatures` — the boolean fold
per occurrence — at **28.16 ms** for 8 occurrences. Against the 1 142.49 ms of
edge enumeration for the same count:

| Component of a patterned blend, 8 occurrences | Cost | Share |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| **Edge enumeration** (`applyBlendOccurrence` → `edgesOf`) | **1 142.49 ms** | **97.6 %** |
| Boolean fold (`recomputeAllFeatures`) | 28.16 ms | 2.4 % |

**97.6 % of a patterned blend's measured cost is the §6.5 defect**, and it is
paid once per occurrence. The two worst-scaling behaviours in the codebase
compose, and nothing in the UI suggests to a user that patterning a fillet is
what triggers it.

Multiplying the measured per-occurrence cost out to larger bodies, using the
k = 2.012 exponent of §6.5:

| Body | `allEdges` per occurrence | × 8 occurrences | × 16 occurrences |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 180 edges | **142.9 ms (measured)** | **1.14 s (measured)** | 2.29 s |
| 360 edges | 578 ms | 4.6 s | 9.3 s |
| 720 edges | 2 340 ms | 18.7 s | 37.4 s |

**What is still not measured:** `applyBlendOccurrence` end to end. A blend
source needs a `BodyModifyFeature` whose edge fingerprints resolve against a
live solid, and a fixture that failed to resolve would report a fast zero — the
M212 failure. The dominant term (97.6 %) and the boolean fold are both measured;
what remains unmeasured is cloning the feature and re-anchoring the
fingerprints, which the decomposition above bounds at a few per cent.

**Verdict: PROBLEM — measured, not derived.** 142.9 ms per occurrence, exactly
linear, of which 97.6 % is edge enumeration.

**Verdict for the pattern arithmetic itself: SMOOTH throughout.** The largest
pattern cost measured is 0.0213 ms.
The along-a-curve variant costs ≈ 14× the straight case and remains 21 µs.
**The cost of a part pattern therefore lies entirely in the kernel** — which is
precisely what the mirror scenario was constructed to establish, and
`kernel.mirror` (§6.6) quantifies the part that is not free.

*Behavioural contract, pinned by test:* a pattern of count *n* yields *n − 1*
placements; the identity placement is discarded (`part_model.dart:3370`)
because it **is** the original feature.

## 9. Threats to validity and unmeasured quantities

### 9.1 Structural gaps

1. **Interior of the C++ shim.** `edgeInfo` is established as Θ(shape) and the
   four responsible operations are identified by reading. No per-line profile
   exists. §6.5 bounds it from outside; from Dart nothing further is possible.
2. **RealityKit’s render loop.** `RvPerf` measures to hand-off only.
3. **No sampling profiler.** The suite attributes cost to *operations*; a
   profiler attributes it to *lines*. For `analyzeSketch` this is the
   difference between “rank reduction is cubic” and “this loop is”. Specified
   as Track A4 of the retired measurement plan; see §15.5. Not built.
4. **`applyBlendOccurrence` end to end.** Its dominant term is now measured
   (`app.blendPattern.edgeQuery.*`, M221); the function as a whole is not,
   because a blend fixture that failed to resolve its edges would report a
   fast zero. See §8.2.
5. ~~**End-to-end rebuild of a `PatternFeature`**~~ **Closed and measured
   (M223)** — `app.patternRebuild.{2,4,8}` drives one through
   `recomputeAllFeatures`: **16.20 / 13.70 / 28.16 ms**, k = 0.40, i.e.
   dominated by fixed cost rather than by occurrence count. Set against the
   1 142.49 ms of edge enumeration at the same count, the boolean fold is
   **2.4 %** of a patterned blend (§8.2).
6. **Ribbon leaf widgets and dialogs.** Frequency matters more than duration
   here, and `menu.ribbon.builds` = 1 makes them uninteresting at present.
7. ~~**Undo journal size.**~~ **Closed (M221)** — `app.journal.depth`,
   `.bytes` and `.bytesPerEntry` are now published by `app.history.*`. The
   journal is unbounded by design (`app_state.dart:415`, "undo until the
   start, nothing gets lost"); that choice is not challenged here, but its
   consequence is now visible. `journalBytes` is an explicit **estimate** —
   Dart exposes no retained size, so it counts 8 bytes per geometry double,
   2 per UTF-16 code unit of the serialised strings, and a flat 64 per
   snapshot and per `Geo`. Wrong in absolute terms, correct in shape, which
   is what a growth question needs. **Read `bytesPerEntry`**: constant means
   the journal grows linearly with edits, rising means each snapshot is also
   getting bigger.

   **First device numbers (M223), reference arm:** `depth` = 21,
   `bytes` = 601 860, **`bytesPerEntry` = 28 660**. So a 21-edit session
   carries roughly **0.6 MB** of undo journal, at ≈ 28 KB per entry. Against a
   footprint measured in gigabytes this is negligible *at this depth*, and the
   estimate is deliberately crude (§ above). What it does not yet answer is the
   question the gauge exists for: **whether `bytesPerEntry` is constant.** One
   observation cannot show a trend. A session with several hundred edits would
   settle it, and until then the unbounded-growth risk is unquantified rather
   than excluded.

### 9.2 A defect in the apparatus

**Failure causes could not reach the bundle — fixed, pending confirmation.**
`kernel.sweepTwist.fail` = 4, and M216 added `lastError` logging expressly so
the next run would report *why*. It was absent, and the ordering explains it:
`log.txt` terminates at 10:47:45 with “BUG REPORT REQUESTED”, while the suite
executed at 10:48:10 — **25 seconds after the log was captured**. The
diagnostic was written after the snapshot intended to carry it and therefore
could never appear.

**Fix (M221):** `Perf.note(name, text)` carries short free-text findings in the
suite's own JSON, which is assembled after the suite by construction, so the
ordering that lost the log entry cannot lose these. The kernel guard now
records `kernel.<op>.fail.reason` alongside `kernel.<op>.fail`, and
`ci/perf_report.py` prints the reason directly beneath the counter it explains.
Notes keep the **first** occurrence rather than the last, because when an
operation fails repeatedly the informative failure is the one that started it.

**Status: CONFIRMED WORKING (2026-08-18).** Both arms of the paired run carry
`kernel.sweepTwist.fail.reason = "occt_sweep_profile: twist is not implemented
yet"`. The question was open across three device runs; the channel answered it
on its first outing, and the answer is that the null was never a defect. See
§3.3. **This item is closed.**

### 12.5 What the longitudinal record establishes

1. **The `allEdges` defect is stable across four builds and two device
   states.** Exponent 1.92–2.012, per-call cost reproducing to 0.8 %, and the
   1-second edge threshold reproducing to 0.5 % from two independent tiers. It
   is safe to work on: any change will show against a well-characterised
   baseline.
2. **Exact counters reproduce perfectly where timings do not.** 120 solves per
   60 frames in all runs; edge counts exact everywhere; `analyze.dof = 1022`
   matching the algebra exactly (§5.5.2). This is the empirical case for §1.1's
   rule that a counter is stronger evidence than a duration.
3. **The solver mechanism is stable, its magnitude is not.** 0.4 % and 0.34 %
   of time in libslvs when over-constrained, across two builds — but 334× and
   245× end-to-end, because that depends on the fixture's distance from
   satisfiability. Quote the share, not the ratio.
4. **One run's numbers were partly fiction and it took a dedicated coverage
   test to find out.** Run 1 reported three plausible small numbers that were
   measurements of nothing. Everything since asserts that a scenario reached
   its subject before believing its timing.
5. **Ramps predict, stress ladders confirm.** The ramp tier flagged rising
   local exponents in `analyzeSketch` at 192–256 entities and called cubic row
   reduction as the likely cause; the stress tier, reaching 1024, measured
   k = 3.198. Extrapolation from a ramp is a hypothesis, and this one survived
   its test.
6. **More data corrected two claims, both in the direction of narrowing.** The
   Low Power Mode scalar is stratified, not uniform (§3.5); and the 3D push path
   costs 0.097 ms, not the 8.8 ms its round-trip span reports (§7.2.4). Both
   errors came from small samples — four spans and one observation respectively.

## 13. Track B — the iOS Simulator

### 13.1 Why this section was empty for the whole branch

`sim-perf.yml` builds the entire native stack for `iphonesimulator/x86_64`,
builds the Flutter app against it, boots an iPad simulator, runs the app and
retrieves `performance_logs.txt` and `performance_snapshot.json` from the data
container. It has been green since run 32 (6 Aug).

**Not one of its numbers had ever been read.** The measurement worked; the
*delivery* did not. GitHub artifacts are served from Azure blob storage, which
the agent proxy refuses (HTTP 403 on the CONNECT tunnel), so the capture was
reachable only by a human downloading a zip. Item 1 of `PERF_ANALYSIS.md` §7 —
"run sim-perf once, pull `perf-capture`" — had therefore stood open since M215
while the job that satisfies it ran green a dozen times.

This is worth recording as a methodological failure in its own right: **a
measurement with no delivery path is not a measurement.** It is the same class
of defect as §9.2, where a diagnostic is written 25 seconds after the snapshot
meant to carry it.

### 13.2 The fix

The workflow now publishes its capture to a **`ci-logs-perf` branch** as well
as an artifact, reusing the pattern the repository already uses for
`ci-debug-logs-dart`, `-m3` and `-m5`. The files are small (one text log and
two JSON), so a branch is a cheap and durable channel, and git is reachable
from every environment that needs to read them. A `RUN.txt` stamps run id,
commit and timestamp, because two simulator captures are otherwise
indistinguishable.

### 13.3 What these numbers may and may not be used for

Established in `PERF_ANALYSIS.md` §6a and unchanged. Flutter refuses
`--profile` and `--release` for the simulator, so the engine is JIT/debug:

| Legitimate to read | **Not** legitimate to read |
| --- | --- |
| `ffi.occt.*`, `ffi.slvs.*`, `ffi.qcad.*` **relative to each other** | fps, `frame.build`, `frame.raster`, jank |
| **All counters** — call counts, entity counts, solves per frame, cache hit rates | absolute milliseconds as iPad milliseconds |
| Structural regressions ("does it now call this twice as often?") | anything about rendering |

The native half is built `-DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release`, exactly as for the
device, so kernel *ratios* transfer. The Dart half is unoptimised and its
frame times are meaningless. Quoting a simulator millisecond as an iPad
millisecond repeats the M75 error in new clothing.

A second, structural caveat specific to this track: the whole stack runs
**x86_64 under Rosetta** on an arm64 runner, because the Qt-for-iOS package
ships only an x86_64 simulator slice. So this is not merely a different clock
from the device — it is a different instruction set, executed under binary
translation. Relative kernel costs survive that; nothing else reliably does.

**This is the decisive comparison of the whole track.** Low Power Mode applies
a uniform scalar and is therefore a usable proxy for slower hardware. The
simulator does not: its slowdown varies by a factor of 63 between operations.
There is no single number by which a simulator millisecond can be converted
into a device millisecond, and §13.3's prohibition — asserted as policy since
M215 — is now a measurement.

A fixed-overhead model was tested and **rejected**: regressing simulator on
device time gives `sim = 87.2 ms + 190.1 × device` with **R² = 0.095**, i.e. no
useful relationship. So the spread is not a constant per-call cost that could
be subtracted away.

**Structure within the spread.** Grouping by which layer an operation lives in:

| Layer | n | Median ratio | Range |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Native C++ (built `Release` on **both** sides) | 6 | **55×** | 27–900× |
| Dart / Flutter / platform channel | 14 | **221×** | 50–1677× |

The native half degrades about 4× less than the Dart half, which is the
direction the build configuration predicts: the kernels are compiled Release
identically for both targets, while the simulator's Dart engine is JIT/debug
and the whole stack runs **x86_64 under Rosetta** on an arm64 runner. The
ranges overlap and n is small, so this is a consistent tendency rather than an
established law. The rank correlation between an operation's device cost and
its ratio is ρ = −0.50 (n = 20, marginally significant), consistent with a
floor effect inflating the ratios of the cheapest operations — the 900× on
`qcad_version()`, a 6 µs operation on device, should be read that way rather
than as a property of the kernel.

### 13.8 What Track B is actually good for

Narrower than the plan hoped, and now on evidence rather than assertion:

* **Exact counters.** `ffi.qcad.allGeometry.entities` = 3,
  `tabbar.rows.miss` = 1, `toolbar.setItems.calls` = 1. Counters are integers
  and identical in meaning on both platforms; a change in call *count* is a
  real structural regression wherever it is observed.
* **Linkage and startup integrity.** The capture proves the native stack
  linked and initialised — `occt smoke` and the qcad probes all completed. That
  is the question the eleven failed runs of M209–M214 were actually about.
* **Nothing quantitative about time.** With a 63× spread there is no ratio
  worth quoting, not even between subsystems, unless both are measured in the
  same layer and neither is near the quantization floor.

The honest conclusion is that Track B, as currently built, is a **build and
linkage smoke test that also produces numbers**, not a performance track. Its
one measurement of lasting value is §13.5's refutation of the idea that it
could be one.

### 14.2 The pattern that repeated

Four new kernel entry points arrived **unmeasured**: `occt_delete_faces`,

bound, the sweep would have been measuring the cap rather than the tolerance,
and the numbers would have looked flat and reassuring for the wrong reason.

### 15.4 The regression gate

Every number in this document is a snapshot. Until M224 nothing *detected* a
regression — the tooling could compare two bundles when a person pointed it at
both, which is a reading aid, not a gate. `ci/perf_gate.py` is the gate:

```
python3 ci/perf_gate.py --record <bundle.zip>   # freeze a baseline
python3 ci/perf_gate.py <bundle.zip>            # compare; exit 1 if worse
```

The committed baseline is the **reference arm** of the paired run — build
`230f179`, Low Power Mode off, thermal nominal at both ends, all three runners:
273 spans, 373 scenario-spans, 46 counters, 195 gauges.

#### Where it departs from the original plan, and why

The measurement plan's gate item ("B4", now retired — §15.5) specified "one entry per scenario with p50/p95, red at >10 % worse". Two of
those three would misfire against what has since been measured:

| B4 said | The gate does | Because |
| --- | --- | --- |
| p50 / p95 | n, total, mean | Percentiles come from a 128-sample ring buffer (§1.1) and for `rv.native.*` are computed over synthesised copies of a mean (§7.2.1). Means are exact. |
| flat 10 % | the run's **own measured** noise floor, per family | `quality.variance.*.spreadPct` gives 4 % for extrude but 29 % for solve and 200 % for splineEval *on the same run* (§3.2). One threshold cannot serve both. A 10 % gate misses real extrude regressions and fires constantly on solve. |
| per scenario | **per scenario, kept** | This part was right — see below. |

#### The evidence ordering, taken from §1.1

The gate checks the strongest evidence first, which inverts the intuitive order:

1. **Counters** — exact integers, invariant under a change of processor. Any
   change fails. Zero false positives from noise. This is where "the painter
   now solves twice per frame" would appear.
2. **Gauges** — exact fixture sizes and ladder ceilings. A changed fixture size
   fails, because it means the durations are not measuring the same work; a
   *fallen* `maxSize` fails, because the app no longer reaches as far.
3. **Durations** — gated last and most softly, against the measured floor,
   and only for spans resolved above the quantization floor (§1.2).

#### Two design decisions the data forced

**It refuses to compare durations across clock states.** Low Power Mode scales
this app by 1.9253, and not uniformly (§3.5). Comparing a capped run against
the uncapped baseline would report a ~93 % regression in everything. The gate
says so and checks counters and gauges anyway — which is exactly why those are
the primary tier.

**It gates per scenario, not only per span.** This was measured, not assumed: a
40 % regression injected into `ffi.occt.allEdges` in one scenario moved the
whole-app aggregate for that span by **6.9 %**, under any usable floor, because
the span also runs inside ramps, fillet scenarios and the blend pattern. The
aggregate alone would have passed it. Per scenario it shows at its full 40 %.

#### What the exclusion list is, and how it was derived

The hard tiers only work if a changed gauge really does mean the fixture
changed. Many gauges are not fixture descriptors — some are measurements in
disguise, some describe the open document, some are cumulative. These were not
guessed at: **the two arms of the paired run executed identical fixtures on the
same build**, so any gauge differing between them cannot be describing fixture
size. 125 do, and classifying all of them yields exactly four rules:

| Class | Count | Example |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Fitted local exponents (`ramp.*.k.*`) | 57 | `ramp.analyze.k.64` = 510 — a measurement ×100 |
| Cumulative solver-path counters | 48 | `ramp.solve.path.slvs.16` |
| Native-drain worsts (reset per capture) | 9 | `rv.native.planes.worstUs` |
| Derived `quality.*` results | 5 | `quality.budget.entitiesAt60Hz` |
| The open document, not the fixtures | 4 | `features`, `triangles` |

That leaves exactly two gateable gauges differing between the arms —
`stress.allEdges.maxSize` (480 → 240) and `stress.allEdges.edges` (1440 → 720)
— which differ because the ladder genuinely did not reach as far under the
capped clock. Running the gate on the capped arm reports those two plus the
counter that follows from them (`ffi.occt.edgeInfo.calls`, −1440) **and nothing
else**, on a run whose durations were entirely unusable. That is the design
working: a real capability regression caught through clock-invariant channels.

#### Verification

25 of the 45 tests in `ci/test_perf_tools.py` cover the gate, and roughly half
of those assert that something does **not** fire — a gate that cries wolf gets
switched off, and is then worse than no gate because everyone believes it is
watching. The committed baseline is itself checked: it must parse, must
round-trip against its own gate, and must have been recorded uncapped at
thermal nominal, since a capped baseline would silently disable duration
checking forever.

#### What it does not yet do

- **It has never run against a second device bundle**, because none exists
  after the baseline. Its first real use is the next device capture.
- **It does not run in CI on every push**, because CI cannot produce a device
  bundle. The natural next step is to point the counter and gauge tiers at the
  simulator capture on `ci-logs-perf` (§13.3 establishes counters *are*
  legitimate to read from the simulator, and §13.8 that structural regression
  detection is what Track B is good for). That needs a simulator baseline and
  is not built.
- **It does not gate exponents.** A change in *k* is the most transferable
  regression of all, but fitting requires a whole family and the confidence
  intervals are wide (§1.5); a naive comparison would fire on noise.

### 15.5 The measurement plan, retired — what survives it

`PERF_PLAN.md` was the experimental design written before any of this was
measured. Its Phase 1 is built and its findings are this document; Phase 4's
gate is §15.4. The rest was superseded by what the measurements actually found,
and the file has been retired rather than left to rot as a plan that no longer
describes the work. Four things in it are still live and are recorded here so
they survive the deletion.

#### The optimisation discipline

Written after M75, where three rounds of optimisation went into the wrong layer
while the stutter was in the 2D painter. It is worth more now than when it was
written, because optimisation is about to start:

1. **Never optimise without a scenario name.** "Feels slow" is not admissible;
   "`stress.analyze` at 1024 entities, 8 837 ms" is.
2. **Walk the attribution ladder in order:** `frame.total` → build (Dart) or
   raster (GPU)? → which named span holds the largest share of the wall clock?
   → is the leaf Dart or native?
3. **Record a baseline before every change** — now mechanised (§15.4), at
   thermal `nominal`, with Low Power Mode off.
4. **One change, then the same measurement again**, confirmed on the device.
   A win that does not appear on-device was not on the critical path.
5. **The delta goes in the milestone note**, as `HANDOFF.md` carries everything
   else.

#### Two instruments specified and never built

- **The sampling profiler** (VM Service `getCpuSamples` → Perfetto), plan item
  A4. The suite says which *operation* costs what; a profiler says which
  *line*. For `analyzeSketch` that is the difference between "rank reduction is
  cubic" (§5.5.2, established) and "this loop is". Still the largest unbuilt
  piece of apparatus.
- **Headless kernel benchmarks** (plan Lane C). The C++ under `backend/` needs
  no iOS: a `bench` target against the C-ABI shim with fixed fixtures, run on
  `macos-14` for the same ISA family, gives minutes-per-loop iteration on
  exactly the operations §6.5 indicts. This is the fastest loop available for
  the `occt_shape_edge_info` work specifically, and it does not exist.

#### The scenario the catalogue always skips

The plan's catalogue had 18 entries; 17 are covered by the suite. **Number 18
is the 30-minute continuous session**, and it is the only one that can detect a
leak. A CAD session is an hour, not a click. The paired run's 293 s is the
longest yet (§3.4) and is not a substitute.

#### What is genuinely not obtainable, so nobody spends a week on it

| Wanted | Why not |
| --- | --- |
| GPU utilisation as a percentage | No iOS API exposes it to a sandboxed app. `frame.raster` and the presented cadence are the honest proxies. |
| Metal frame capture / per-draw GPU time on device | Requires Xcode attached. |
| Instruments against the physical iPad | Requires a Mac. Simulator only, in CI. |
| Xcode Organizer field data | Requires App Store / TestFlight distribution; sideloaded builds never report. |
| Energy per subsystem | Not exposed. Thermal state plus CPU time per thread is the closest available. |

### 15.1 What the suite does NOT produce

The self-driving suite builds its own fixtures, so its ~110 scenarios fire
regardless of what is on screen. **Forty spans do not come from the suite at
all** — they only exist if the app was actually used before the bug button was
pressed. In the 11 August run they were populated because the session happened
to include that use; nothing guarantees it.

| To get these spans | Do this first |
| --- | --- |
| `rv.native.*` (11 spans — the entire RealityKit drain, §7.2), `rv.setScene/setOverlays/setCamera`, `3d.push`, `3d.payload` | **Open a part and look at it in 3D.** Orbit once. Without this the origin-plane finding cannot be re-checked at all. |
| `menu.ribbon.part`, `browser.sig`, `toolbar.sig`, `tabbar.sig` | Switch to the Part tab, open the model browser, use the toolbar. |
| `io.savePart` | Save a document. |
| `2d.paint.*`, `2d.snap`, `2d.pickEntity`, `2d.displayGeometry` at REAL sizes | Draw a few entities and drag one. The suite measures these on fixtures; the session measures them on whatever you actually drew. |
| `launch.toFirstFrame` and the 21 `step.*` spans | Automatic — but only from a **cold start**. Resuming a backgrounded app leaves them from the previous launch. |

**Practical consequence:** a run where the bug button is pressed immediately
after launch produces a complete *suite* and an almost empty *session*. Both
halves matter — §4b of the report is the session, and it is the only place the
app's real usage appears.

### 15.2 How to take it — two runs, in this order

Order matters: **the stress run can kill the app**, and an app killed by
jetsam writes no bundle at all. Taking the safe run first guarantees at least
one complete capture.

**Build:** anything newer than `230f179`. Older binaries have no `Perf.note`,
no journal gauges and none of the five new scenario families.

---

**Run 1 — Low Power Mode ON, with `stress`.**

1. Turn Low Power Mode **on**. Let the device settle until it is cool.
2. **Cold-start the app** (swipe it away first) — otherwise the 21 `step.*`
   spans and `launch.toFirstFrame` carry over from a previous launch.
3. **Use it for a minute:** open a part, look at it in 3D and orbit once,

## 17. Round one optimisation — measured

Sections 1–16 measure the application **before** any optimisation. This section
reports what five parallel sessions changed, adjudicated against predictions
each of them registered **before writing code**, by a paired device capture on
build `b2de0c2` (runs 7 and 8, §12.6).

**Sections 1–16 are not rewritten.** They are the derivation, and a superseded

### 17.1 The two results that matter

#### `analyzeSketch` — the cubic is gone

| | Before (run 6) | After (run 7) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Fitted exponent | **3.198** [2.835, 3.561] | **2.285** [2.076, 2.495] |
| R² | 0.9962 | 0.9975 |
| 1024 entities | 8 837 ms | **562 ms** (15.7×) |
| 512 entities | 542 ms | 96 ms (5.6×) |
| 256 entities | 76 ms | 18 ms (4.2×) |
| 128 entities | 10 ms | 4 ms (2.5×) |

**The confidence intervals do not overlap.** This is a change of complexity
class, not a constant factor — the only such change in either round. §5.5.2
identified the cubic as `_rankAndPivots` reducing a dense `m × total` matrix by
Gaussian elimination, O(m·total·rank); a sparse elimination replaced it. The
speed-up growing with size (2.5× → 15.7×) is the signature of the exponent
moving rather than the constant.

Dropping the 64-entity rung, which sits at the 1 µs quantum and cannot be
resolved, gives k = 2.382 [2.121, 2.642] — the same conclusion.

#### `allEdges` — 21× cheaper, and still quadratic

| | Before (run 6) | After (run 7) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Fitted exponent | 2.012 [1.910, 2.113] | **1.930** [1.876, 1.984] |
| 480 profile points | 10 017 ms | **474 ms** (21.1×) |
| 240 profile points | 2 508 ms | 125 ms (20.1×) |
| 120 profile points | 616 ms | 35 ms (17.6×) |
| Ladder reach | 480 pts (1 440 edges) | **1 920 pts (5 760 edges)** |
| 960 / 1920 points | unreachable | 1 845 ms / 7 320 ms |

**The intervals overlap, so the exponent did not move.** The bulk entry point
`occt_shape_edges_info` replaced one FFI call per edge with one per shape —
`ffi.occt.edgeInfo.calls` **8 316 → 100**, with 44 bulk calls covering 16 956
edges — and bought a factor of 21 on the constant and **four times the reachable
model size**. It did not remove the Θ(n²).

This was **predicted before the device ran**. Lane C, the headless kernel bench
built by another session in the same round, measured k = 1.909 on CI hardware
and refuted the prediction days early; the device returned 1.930. §6.5's
mechanism was also refined: the residual quadratic is not the solid classifier
but a `TopExp_Explorer` over each adjacent face's edges, run once per edge.

**The capped arm confirms both, so neither is a clock artefact:** 17.2× at 120
points and 19.7× at 240, with reach going **240 → 1920 points, an 8× increase**.

### 17.3 The predictions, adjudicated

Every session registered its predictions with the arithmetic deriving them,
before changing code. One capture settled all of them.

| Prediction | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| **`2d.paint` 0.167527 → 0.11057 ms** | **HELD to 2.1 %.** Measured 0.112900. |
| `2d.displayGeometry.solves` 120 → 60 | **HELD exactly.** Plus a new `cacheHit = 60`. |

**A prediction that comes out wrong is the most valuable kind**, and the

refuted by a bench built in the same round, *before* the device capture, which
is exactly what that bench was built to be able to do. Without it, a 21× cost
drop would have been reported as success and the surviving quadratic missed.

### 17.4 An optimisation that revealed a cost it had been hiding

§6.3 and §10.2 recorded `kernel.fillet.edges` as **independent of edge count** —
25.5 ms whether one edge was filleted or twelve, k = 0.00, reproduced under both
clocks and across two builds. That was true, and it was an artefact.

The flat total was the candidate search (`allEdges`) dominating. With the search
12× cheaper, the blend's own cost is visible for the first time:

| Edges filleted | 1 | 4 | 12 | fitted *k* |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Before — whole scenario | 25.59 | 25.56 | 25.58 | **0.00**, R² 0.15 |
| After — `ffi.occt.filletEdges` | **5.21** | **10.67** | **24.14** | **0.613**, R² 0.9892 |

**It was never flat; it was masked.** A measurement that reproduces perfectly
across builds and clock states can still be measuring the wrong thing, and the
only reason this surfaced is that something upstream of it got faster. Worth
holding against every other flat line in §10.

### 17.5 Validity

**The device did not change.** The Low Power Mode scalar, re-derived from 135
matched spans in this run, is **1.888** [1.870, 1.907] against round one's
1.925 [1.898, 1.953] over 142 spans — overlapping intervals. A scalar that had
moved would mean the hardware, not the code, explained the results.

Thermal `nominal` at all four probe points across both arms. Both captures on
build `b2de0c2`, the commit pinned as `perf-capture-round1` and the exact commit
of the green IPA build 438.

**Three genuine regressions**, all just over the measured noise floor and all
recorded rather than dismissed: `ffi.occt.meshCreate` +13.6 %, `ramp.mesh.12`
+10.6 %, `ramp.solve.24` +13.5 %. None is explained. They are small, they are
above the floor, and they are the kind of thing that compounds if ignored.

**`ffi.occt.allEdges` is still the largest single line item at 35.1 % of suite
time** — but only because the ladder now climbs to 1 920 profile points. At
matched size it is 21× cheaper. Reading that table without the reach change
would invert its meaning.

### 17.6 What round one did not fix

- **The quadratic itself** (§17.1). Cheaper by 21×, same exponent. This is the
  single largest piece of unfinished work and it has a session assigned.
- **The drift**: dragging a sketch around a closed loop leaves it **14.64 units**
  from where it started, in the unmodified application. Found incidentally while
  proving something else, and out of scope for a branch whose rule was that
  behaviour does not change. It is a correctness question, not a performance one.
- **The display path**: 419.67 ms first scene push, 99.95 % origin planes.
  Untouched.
- **Memory**: still the unproxied axis, still where the app died in the field.
